Yes, [India](https://www.google.com/search?kgmid=/m/03rk0) mandates data compliance for healthcare, primarily driven by the [Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/dpdp-in-healthcare-ecosystem). While India does not use the American "HIPAA" framework, it enforces strict personal data governance rules that heavily impact hospitals, labs, and health-tech applications. [1, 2, 3, 4] 
The primary regulations and compliance pillars shaping India's healthcare data landscape include the following elements:
## 1. The DPDP Act, 2023 (The Core Mandate)
The [DPDP Act](https://kpmg.com/in/en/insights/2025/12/the-privacy-prescription-impact-of-dpdp-act-and-rules-in-healthcare-and-life-sciences-sector.html) acts as a horizontal data protection law. Because healthcare information is highly sensitive, medical facilities and health platforms are under intense scrutiny: [2, 4, 5, 6, 7] 

* Explicit Consent: Hospitals and apps cannot rely on blanket or implied consent. Patients must receive clear notices specifying exactly what data is collected and why. [5, 8] 
* Purpose Limitation: If a lab collects a blood sample for a diagnostic test, it cannot use that patient's phone number for marketing or share the data with third parties without separate approval. [5, 8] 
* Right to Erasure: Patients can request that their digital medical profiles be permanently deleted or anonymised once their active treatment timeline concludes. [2, 6] 
* Mandatory Breach Reporting: Any data compromise must be reported to the Data Protection Board of India and the affected individuals within a strictly defined window. [4, 5] 

## 2. DISHA (Digital Information Security in Healthcare Act)
While the DPDP Act covers all personal data, [DISHA](https://compliancy-group.com/disha-and-hipaa-how-do-they-compare/) functions specifically as a healthcare data framework. It establishes "Privacy by Design" principles to regulate how digital health data is generated, stored, and transmitted, completely prohibiting the commercial exploitation of patient data. [2, 9] 
## 3. Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM)
To modernise healthcare infrastructure, the government introduced the ABDM ecosystem. This introduces: [2, 10, 11] 

* ABHA ID: A unique health account number linking a patient's records across different hospitals seamlessly.
* Consent Manager Architecture: Interoperable digital plumbing that allows patients to digitally approve or revoke a doctor's access to their medical records in real-time. [2, 12, 13, 14, 15] 

## 4. Professional Codes (NMC Guidelines)
The National Medical Commission (NMC) enforces traditional doctor-patient confidentiality codes. A doctor who shares a patient's clinical photos or medical history without explicit authorization faces professional misconduct charges and losing their license to practice. [16, 17, 18] 
------------------------------
## Direct Comparison: HIPAA (US) vs. DPDP Act (India)

| Feature | HIPAA (United States) | DPDP Act (India) |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Healthcare-specific (PHI) | Broad personal data, stricter for health |
| Key Terminology | Covered Entities & Business Associates | Data Fiduciaries & Data Processors |
| Minor Records | Parental control varies by state law | Mandates parental consent under age 18 |
| Physical Records | Highly detailed physical security laws | Governs physical files if digitized or archived |
| Financial Penalty | Up to $1.9 Million per year | Up to ₹250 Crores for significant breaches |

If you are trying to align an organisation with Indian laws, let me know:

* Are you designing a software platform (SaaS) or managing a physical facility (like a clinic)?
* Do you handle cross-border healthcare data, such as serving overseas telemedicine clients? [1, 4, 19, 20]
