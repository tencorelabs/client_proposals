HIPAA compliance means protecting sensitive patient health information (Protected Health Information or PHI) under U.S. federal law. It applies to healthcare providers, health plans, clearinghouses (covered entities), and their service vendors (business associates) through strict privacy, security, and breach notification rules. [1, 2, 3] 
## Core Rules of HIPAA

* Privacy Rule: Restricts how patient PHI is used and shared, giving individuals rights to view and correct their health records.
* Security Rule: Mandates safeguards for electronic PHI (ePHI).
* Breach Notification Rule: Requires reporting data compromises to the government and affected individuals.
* Omnibus Rule: Extends direct legal accountability to subcontractors and business partners. [1, 2, 3] 

## Three Types of Safeguards

* Administrative: Policies, risk assessments, designated security officers, and workforce training.
* Physical: Facility access limits, workstation security, and device controls.
* Technical: Access credentials, activity logs, and data encryption. [4, 5]
